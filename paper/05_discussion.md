# Discussion

## What the Graph Adds

The estimator results separate two kinds of demand. The standing queue of an arm is largely a local quantity: its visible part, the fill ratio of the camera zone, the time since the last green and the previous green share predict it well, and a graph-free MLP estimates it as accurately as the GAT. Arrivals over the next two minutes and the flow ratio of a junction are network quantities, because they depend on what the upstream junction is serving and for how long. This is where attention over feeder and trunk edges pays off, with the largest gain on internal arms. The attention map in Figure 16 is consistent with this reading: internal arms attend to the side arms of the neighbouring junction, whose turning movements feed both corridor directions.

Better prediction did not translate into lower waiting time. In the snapshot regime the timing law gained most from cycle adaptation driven by the predicted flow ratio and from replacing a saturated camera score with an estimate of the hidden queue, and every learned estimator delivered both. Across three training seeds the graph-free MLP then gave the lowest waiting time and the GAT a slightly higher one, even though the GAT was the more accurate predictor. Two reasons are plausible. In a split-phased junction, green time is governed by the queue that must be cleared, while arrival forecasts shift the split only at the margin, and the queue is the quantity that local features already predict well. The training loss also weighs absolute PCU errors equally on all arms, whereas the timing law uses ratios between the arms of one junction and the flow ratio of the critical junction, so an error pattern that barely changes the loss can still change the plan. This is the familiar gap between prediction accuracy and decision quality; training the estimator on a loss built from the timing law itself is the natural remedy. A graph estimator earns its cost when arrivals dominate the decision, as on short links or during platoon progression, or when its attention maps are needed for operator audit, and a local learned estimator suffices when standing queues dominate.

## Hidden State Limits Camera-Only Control

The two deep Q-networks isolate the effect of sensing on a learned policy. With the exact simulator state, the network reached a policy within 100 episodes that beat every controller on mean waiting time, although with less even service across arms (Jain index 0.78-0.85 against 0.87-0.99 for GATC). With camera frames, the same network produced policies that worked off-peak and collapsed at peak in all nine configurations, including one with the reward and training settings of DQN-O (549.8 against 84.9 s/veh on the tuning seeds), and a perfect detector (P0) did not repair them. The missing ingredient is information, not detection accuracy: a frame truncates the queue at 60 m, so an arm holding 30 PCU and an arm holding 120 PCU can look alike, and a reward-driven policy trained on such observations learns short greens that suit the states it can distinguish. GATC treats the same problem explicitly. Its estimator is trained to reconstruct the hidden queue and the coming arrivals from the visible frame, the signal history and the neighbours, and its timing law turns that reconstruction into green time with bounds that an engineer can check. On the S1-S5 mean this recovered 94% of the gap between camera-trained and exact-state reinforcement learning; the remaining 4.2-16.1% is the margin that exact sensing gave a learned policy in this corridor.

## Sensing Rate Matters More Than Coordination

The strongest single effect in the study is the tactical layer. Adding the once-per-second check of the 20 m stop-line zone reduced mean waiting time by 31% (GATC against GATC-S), made every controller that used it nearly insensitive to detector quality, and handled the incident through its blocked-flow condition. With that layer in place, a local proportional plan performed within 4% of the graph-coordinated plan. This mirrors the two-level design of SCATS, whose tactical gap-out corrects a strategic plan cycle by cycle, and it agrees with the long-standing advantage of actuated over fixed-time control at junctions with fluctuating demand. The camera version has two advantages over loops in Indian traffic. It does not depend on lane discipline, and it can tell a waiting queue from a stalled one, which presence-mode loops cannot: ACT keeps a blocked green alive until its maximum, while GATC ends it within 4 s.

The sensing budget sets which layer to build first. A UVH-26-class detector such as YOLO11-S costs about 21.6 GFLOP per 640-pixel frame, so the snapshot regime (about 1,220 frames per hour for the corridor) needs roughly 7 GFLOP/s on average, whereas 5,100 stop-line checks per hour with the same detector would need about 31 GFLOP/s more. The tactical check needs only presence, so a nano-sized detector or a cropped zone suffices, and the flat response of GATC and LPS-T across detector profiles in Table 12 shows that class errors do not hurt it. Where cameras are shared with police surveillance, as Safe-City cameras often are, or where a heavy India-specific detector must run on existing CPUs, frames arrive sparsely, and the strategic layer with a learned estimator recovers most of the gap between the local proportional controller and fixed-time plans tuned to true demand.

## Relation to Earlier Studies

Reinforcement learning controllers report reductions relative to fixed timing of similar size to those observed here, such as the 27.34% average cut in waiting time of the federated PPO controller at a single junction [@19]. Those figures come from policies that read exact lane states from a simulator [@2], and the DQN-O result reproduces that finding in this corridor. The DQN-C result adds the camera-only case: the same learner, fed truncated frames, fell behind design-day fixed timing in S2, S3 and S5. GATC reaches reductions of 19.0% to 60.2% relative to design-day fixed time from noisy camera counts, with a supervised estimator whose outputs can be checked against field counts and a timing law that an engineer can read line by line. Attention-based controllers such as CoLight [@12] and AttendLight [@21] use attention inside the policy; in GATC the coefficients serve an estimator with physical targets, so an operator can inspect which neighbours a prediction relied on. The detector results add a caution to vision-based counting studies [@3], [@6]: accuracy on a benchmark does not map directly onto control quality. A learned estimator absorbs a consistent miss rate but reacts to a shift in net count bias, so the detector used in the field should match the detector used to generate training data, and a change of detector calls for recalibration.

## Limitations and Threats to Validity

The evidence comes from simulation. The mesoscopic model reproduces Webster's delay but does not represent lateral movement, two-wheeler creeping into the junction box, pedestrians, bus stops or dilemma-zone behaviour, which is why tactical thresholds were bounded at the 2 s used in practice rather than at the smaller values that tuning preferred. Detector profiles are assumptions scaled to published accuracy gaps and not measurements of best.pt, YOLOv8n and YOLOv8m on UVH-26 frames, although the sweep in Figure 15 covers a wide band around them. The corridor has one geometry, split phasing only and synthetic demand, and the estimator was trained in the same simulator in which it was tested, so its field accuracy will depend on how closely the simulator matches the site. The main comparisons use one training run of each estimator and each DQN configuration; the estimator seed spread in Table 7 (up to 2.6 s/veh) is small next to the differences between controller families, and the checkpoint-to-checkpoint spread of the DQNs is visible in Figure 11. Recurrent or attention-based reinforcement learners with memory of past frames were not tested and may narrow the camera gap. The failure of the snapshot regime during the incident shows that a strategic plan without within-cycle feedback should not run alone on arms where blockages are common; a mid-phase frame that checks for stalled discharge would be the minimal safeguard.

## Ethics and Deployment

The controller consumes counts, not identities. Frames can be processed at the camera, reduced to class counts and zone occupancy, and discarded, so no face, number plate or trajectory needs to be stored. Fairness across arms was monitored with Jain's index and stayed at or above that of actuated control in S1-S4. Deployment should keep a fixed-time fallback that engages when detection fails or the estimator's inputs leave their training range, and the per-junction impact scores $\hat{\psi}_j$ should be displayed to operators, as in the original prototype, so that the automatic plan remains open to human override.
