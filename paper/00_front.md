TITLE: Camera-Only Coordinated Signal Control for Heterogeneous Traffic: Graph Attention Estimation of Hidden Queues with an Auditable Two-Level Timing Law

# Abstract

Roadside cameras at Indian urban junctions see only the first 60 m of an arm, while split phasing and two-wheeler-dominated traffic make every misallocated second of green costly, so snapshot-based controllers misjudge demand exactly when queues are longest. This paper presents GATC, a camera-only controller for multi-junction corridors. YOLO detections in the 14 classes of the UVH-26 dataset become passenger-car-unit (PCU) lane scores on a 15-node graph of arm and junction nodes. A two-layer graph attention network (GAT), trained by supervised learning on vehicle passage logs, predicts the hidden standing queue and next-120 s arrivals of every arm and the flow ratio of every junction. An explicit timing law sets the cycle from Webster's formula, discounts arms that feed loaded links and splits green by predicted demand; a tactical layer checks the stop-line zone once per second and ends idle or blocked greens. The evaluation used a three-junction, dual-lane corridor in a mesoscopic simulator verified against Webster's delay formula (within 2.1% up to a degree of saturation of 0.9), five scenarios, 20 paired seeds, four detector profiles and nine comparison controllers, including deep Q-networks trained on camera frames and on the exact simulator state. The GAT predicted next-cycle demand with R² of 0.84, against 0.61 for a calibrated snapshot and 0.80 for a graph-free estimator. GATC reduced average waiting time by 26.1-55.8% relative to the original local proportional controller and by 19.0-60.2% relative to design-day fixed time, and matched or outperformed loop-actuated and camera max-pressure control in every scenario. The camera-trained deep Q-network collapsed at peak demand (GATC 51.1-69.6% faster), whereas the exact-state network was 4.2-16.1% faster than GATC; GATC recovered 94% of that gap and served arms more evenly. Diagnostics locate the gains: graph attention sharpens prediction and yields auditable attention maps, while closed-loop gains come from hidden-queue estimation, cycle adaptation and the once-per-second stop-line check, which also made GATC insensitive to detector quality.

KEYWORDS: adaptive traffic signal control; graph attention network; heterogeneous traffic; camera-based vehicle detection; UVH-26; hidden queue estimation; deep reinforcement learning; actuated control
