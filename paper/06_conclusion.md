# Conclusion

This paper developed GATC, a camera-only signal controller for split-phased corridors carrying heterogeneous Indian traffic, and evaluated it on a mesoscopic simulator whose delays stayed within 2.1% of Webster's formula up to a degree of saturation of 0.9. Detections with UVH-26 classes become PCU lane scores, a 15-node graph attention network predicts the standing queue and arrivals of every arm and the flow ratio of every junction, and an explicit timing law with an optional once-per-second stop-line check turns these predictions into green time.

Five conclusions follow from 20 paired seeds in five scenarios. First, graph attention predicts next-cycle demand more accurately than a calibrated camera snapshot (R² of 0.84 against 0.61) and than graph-free or neighbourhood-limited estimators, with the largest gains for arrivals on internal arms and for junction flow ratios, but this accuracy did not lower waiting time relative to a graph-free estimator in closed loop. Second, with one set of frames per cycle, the strategic layer cut average waiting time relative to local proportional control by 8.5% to 34.5% in four of five scenarios, mainly through learned queue estimation and cycle adaptation, and failed during a lane blockage because it lacks within-cycle feedback. Third, with the once-per-second check, GATC reduced waiting time by 26.1% to 55.8% relative to the original local controller and by 19.0% to 60.2% relative to design-day fixed time, matched or beat loop-actuated control and camera max-pressure in every scenario, and kept its performance with COCO-trained detectors. Fourth, a deep Q-network trained on the same camera frames collapsed at peak demand while the same network with the exact state was the strongest controller, and GATC recovered 94% of that gap; the hidden queue beyond the camera view, not the choice of learning method, is the binding constraint of camera-only control. Fifth, with the stop-line check in place a local proportional plan came within 4% of the graph plan, so the strategic estimator matters most where frames are sparse, such as shared Safe-City cameras or heavy India-specific detectors on existing CPUs.

The next steps are concrete. The detector profiles and the estimator should be recalibrated on UVH-26 frames processed by the detector that will run in the field, and a pilot on a Bengaluru corridor should compare predicted and counted queues before the timing law is allowed to act. The estimator should be retrained on a loss derived from the timing law, so that accuracy and control quality point the same way, and its hidden-queue predictions can serve as observations for a reinforcement learner. The graph should also be extended to larger networks with pedestrian phases and to layouts with short links or platoon progression, where arrival forecasts, the quantity that graph attention predicts best, carry more weight in the green split.
